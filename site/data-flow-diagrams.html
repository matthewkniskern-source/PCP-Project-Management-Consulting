BUSINESS SYSTEM MODEL

Proposed PCP information flows

These diagrams document the information exchanged among clients, consultants, office personnel, and PCP's proposed internal systems. They model business data, not firewall connections or physical network paths. A context diagram establishes the overall system boundary; the Level 0 diagram decomposes that same system into four major processes and their data stores.

Context diagram — PCP as one system

The context view treats the PCP business system as a single process. Clients submit project requests and receive status information and invoices. Consultants exchange assignments, status updates, and contact information. Office personnel maintain records and receive business reports. An external finance service exchanges payment requests and results. Each of these flows appears in corresponding form in the decomposed view below.

Level 0 diagram — major processes and stores

The Level 0 decomposition separates client information, engagement tracking, billing, and collaboration into distinct processes. It includes four logical data stores; their final physical implementations, database products and retention rules are decisions for the system implementation plan.

Architecture and trust boundaries

The DFD captures the business data that the system needs to process; it does not assert that public visitors can access internal data stores. In the proposed production design, Entra authentication, application-level authorization, private data services and audit logging constrain these exchanges. Administrative PAW/PIM/Bastion pathways are documented separately because they govern privileged infrastructure control rather than the everyday business flows represented here.

Design status: These are conceptual diagrams for the planned PCP modernization. The existing website and employee workspace are public academic demonstrations with fictional records; there is no integrated accounting, communications or protected backend behind the current site. The detailed data model, integrations, software choices and security controls remain subject to implementation.
